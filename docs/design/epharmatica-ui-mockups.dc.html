TOPIC · DIURETICS

Loop diuretics

Loop diuretics inhibit the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ (NKCC2) symporter in the thick ascending limb, abolishing the medullary concentration gradient.

Adverse effects follow the mechanism: ototoxicity is dose-related and compounds with aminoglycosides, while potassium wasting mirrors the increased distal sodium delivery.

MNEMONIC

“OH DANG!” — Ototoxicity, Hypokalemia, Dehydration, Alkalosis, Nephritis, Gout.

Clinically, the ceiling dose rises as renal function falls — titrate to response, not to a fixed maximum.

ASSISTGROQ · LLAMA-4 ▾
Include whole topic≈ 38K TOKENS

Why does the ceiling dose rise in renal impairment?

Loop agents are actively secreted into the tubule by OAT transporters. As GFR falls, competing organic acids accumulate and secretion drops — so a larger plasma dose is needed to reach the same luminal concentration. The material notes this is why you titrate to response.

Loop diuretics

12 MIN READUPDATED 12 JUL 2026SEM 04

Loop diuretics are the most efficacious diuretic class available. They act on the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, where they inhibit the Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ (NKCC2) symporter, abolishing the medullary concentration gradient and producing a brisk, dose-dependent natriuresis.

Because roughly 25% of filtered sodium is reabsorbed at this site, blockade here cannot be fully compensated downstream. Efficacy is preserved even at low glomerular filtration rates, which is why loop agents remain useful where thiazides fail.

CLINICAL PEARL

In renal impairment, higher doses are required to deliver enough drug to the tubular lumen — loop diuretics are secreted, not filtered, into their site of action.

Why does furosemide cause ototoxicity?

FOLLOWING ✓
QUESTIONANSWEREDpharmacologysem-4ASKED 3 DAYS AGO · 12 REPLIES
?Anonymous student (OP)· 3 D AGO

We were told loop diuretics can damage hearing but thiazides can't, even though both act on the nephron. Is it about the dose, the site of action, or something in the inner ear itself?

QuoteReport
SHsana.hVERIFIED· 2 D AGO ✓ ACCEPTED ANSWER
18

The stria vascularis in the inner ear expresses an NKCC co-transporter that maintains the endolymph's potassium gradient. Loop agents inhibit it just as they do NKCC2 in the nephron — so hearing loss is a direct, dose-related extension of the mechanism, not an allergy.

Thiazides act on NCC, which the ear doesn't use — that's the whole difference. Risk rises with rapid IV push and co-administered aminoglycosides.
